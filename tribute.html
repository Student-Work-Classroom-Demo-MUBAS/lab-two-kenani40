Tribute to John Chilembwe

Baptist minister, educator, and forerunner of Malawian independence (c.1871 – 1915)

Biography

John Chilembwe was born around 1871 in the Chiradzulu district of what was then Nyasaland, now Malawi. He received early schooling at a Church of Scotland mission before coming under the influence of the missionary Joseph Booth, who exposed him to ideas of racial equality that were unusual for the colonial era.

In the late 1890s Chilembwe travelled to the United States, where he trained as a minister at a theological college in Virginia. There he was exposed to African-American history and the writings of figures such as Booker T. Washington and Frederick Douglass, which sharpened his sense of injustice under colonial rule. He returned to Nyasaland in 1900 and founded his own independent church and mission stations, building schools alongside them.

By the early 1900s, Chilembwe had become an outspoken critic of the exploitation of African labourers on European-owned plantations and of the colonial government's refusal to give Africans any political voice. In January 1915, shortly after the outbreak of the First World War, he led a short-lived armed uprising against colonial rule. The revolt was defeated within days and Chilembwe was killed while fleeing. He is remembered today as a founding figure of Malawian nationalism, and 15 January is observed annually in Malawi as John Chilembwe Day.

"Strike a blow and die" — words associated with the sermon that launched his 1915 uprising.
